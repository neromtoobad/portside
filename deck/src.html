The problem

On-chain trading feels like a spreadsheet. On-chain games fake the market.

DEX screens are for experts

Pools, slippage and fees, with nothing that tells a newcomer why a price should move or what to do about it.

Trading games don’t trade

Most keep made-up prices on a server. Nothing settles, so nothing teaches you how a real pool behaves.

News games need fair timing

If the operator knows the headline first, the game is rigged. Players have to be able to check who knew what, and when.

The idea

The first stock exchange grew up on a harbour.

In 1600s Amsterdam, a ship coming in, or not, moved the share price of the company that sent it. Portside plays that same game with five stocks people talk about today, each with its own pier.

NVDA chips AAPL phones TSLA cars AMZN parcels META headsets

How a round works

One headline. One ship. One decision.

0:00

News is drawn

The Harbour contract picks a stock, a kind of news and a size: “Earnings beat · NVDA ▲ 12%”.

0:00–0:45

Trade before it docks

Buy before good news, sell before bad. Every trade is a real swap signed in the browser.

0:45

The ship docks

One public house swap moves that pool by exactly the posted amount. Right call, right money.

2:30 · 4:00

Tide, then rumours

Prices drift a quarter of the way back. A minute before the next round, rumour holders hear the next ship.

Reading the news

Six kinds of news. Three push a stock up, two push it down.

Earnings beatProfits came in above forecast
▲ up
Supply stormShipments stuck behind a storm
▼ down
Launch sell-outA new product flying off the shelves
▲ up
Rival fleetCompetitors flooded the port
▼ down
Whale buyingA big fund scooping up shares
▲ up
Calm seasA quiet round on the exchange
no move

The size of each move, 5% to 29%, is on the card. The skill is in timing and sizing, and in selling into the move before the tide pulls it back.

Fair by design

Nobody knows the next ship. Not even us.

Commit, then reveal

The keeper commits to a secret seed a round ahead and must reveal that exact seed to the contract.

Block hash mixed in

The contract mixes the seed with the previous block hash, so the keeper can’t choose or predict a headline.

Docking is one public swap

When a ship lands, one house swap moves the pool by exactly the posted amount. Anyone can check its size.

Rumours don’t change the draw

A ticket only reveals the contract’s next draw a minute early. Standings are anchored daily as a Merkle root.

entropy = keccak256(seed, blockhash(block.number − 1), season, round) → stock, kind, size
